lens-diff reading: the pole is real (7 and 13 markers, consistency 1.0) and the preregistered null is the weak part, not the signal: centred sign flips keep the per-pair
variation in size of the French-English signal, so one flip pattern can leave a signal-aligned residue. Not re-cut; a next prereg would use a null that breaks the pairing
(shuffle English twins across pairs) and say it was chosen after this miss. The first-run miss was my marker list (see `docs/rules.md`).
CAST reading: language is linearly easy (7 of 8 layers give CV 1.0), tie rule picked layer 3. The out-of-domain miss says the cosine threshold fitted on short
translated sentences fires on 12% of English statements at layer 3; the condition direction is not specific to French. Next prereg (not run): pick layer and tau with the
statements as extra negatives. Limits: request-level gate only (one decision per prompt); per-token gating needs an engine change. Effect of a gated persona through
the engine on generations (tier 2) is UNRUN.

Tuned lens reading: all six layers pass, and the affine part does work beyond a constant bias (b-only already cuts KL by 20 to 60%, tuned by a further 24 to 37%). Caveats, none a bar:
the data is the narrow lie-statement capture (45.7k tokens, English encyclopedic statements), 256 held-out positions; the training vocabulary S (4096 tokens) covers 0.75 of the
teacher's mass; the chosen learning rate 3e-5 is the top of the grid, so a higher one may fit better; train KL (0.44 to 2.3) is below held-out validation KL at the deeper layers
(L27 0.44 vs 0.65), a mild gap with 2 epochs; the shuffled-teacher mutant still beats the logit lens by 0.8 at 2 of 6 layers (it learns the token prior), which is why bar 3 against
b-only is the control that matters. Sanity of the apply op (`nx tlens '{"op":"lens",...}'`): at L27 the tuned lens puts ` The`, ` These`, ` A` where the logit lens puts CJK tokens.
